Unit 2 Exam Notes

Polynomials, no calculator. Everything the exam asks you to do, with a quick example of each.

  1. 1. Factor first. It shows you the zeros, the multiplicities and the sign of the leading term.
  2. 2. Zeros tell you how it touches. Odd multiplicity crosses. Even multiplicity bounces.
  3. 3. Degree and sign tell you the ends. Then check one point.

Zeros and multiplicity

A factor (x − r)m gives a zero at x = r with multiplicity m (the exponent).

  • Odd m: crosses. Higher odd (3, 5) flattens as it crosses.
  • Even m: bounces (touches and turns around). Higher even (4, 6) flattens at the touch.
Factor out the common factor first. 3x² − x³ = x²(3 − x), so the zero at 0 has multiplicity 2 (bounce) and the zero at 3 has multiplicity 1 (cross).
Watch (a − x). (3 − x) = −(x − 3). That hidden negative flips the leading coefficient.

End behavior

Only the highest-power term matters: its degree (even or odd) and the sign of its coefficient.

Limit notation (read: as x goes to the right / left, what does f(x) do?)
Odd, positive:  limx→−∞ f(x) = −∞,  limx→∞ f(x) = ∞
Even, negative: limx→−∞ f(x) = −∞, limx→∞ f(x) = −∞
Example
f(x) = −2x⁵ + 7x² − 1: odd degree, negative leading coefficient, so it falls to the right and rises to the left. limx→∞ f(x) = −∞ and limx→−∞ f(x) = ∞.

Graph to equation

  1. Write a factor (x − r) for each zero.
  2. Raise it to its multiplicity: 1 if it crosses, 2 if it bounces.
  3. Put an unknown a in front.
  4. Use a point (usually the y-intercept) to solve for a.
Example
Crosses at −1, bounces at 2, passes through (0, −4).
f(x) = a(x + 1)(x − 2)²
f(0) = a(1)(4) = 4a = −4, so a = −1.
f(x) = −(x + 1)(x − 2)²
Matching a graph to a formula. Check zeros, then multiplicity (cross or bounce), then end behavior. Two of those usually kills three of the four choices.
Unknown letters. If f(x) = a(x − b)(x − c)², the squared factor is the bounce and the other one is the cross. A bounce at −3 means (x + 3)², so c = −3.

Sketching a polynomial

Steps: zeros and behavior, end behavior, y-intercept, then connect smoothly.

f(x) = −(x + 2)(x − 1)²
  • Degree 3, leading coefficient −1: left up, right down.
  • Cross at −2. Bounce at 1.
  • y-intercept: f(0) = −(2)(1) = −2.

Even, odd, or neither

Replace x with −x, simplify, and compare with f(x).

  • Even: f(−x) = f(x) (symmetric over the y-axis).
  • Odd: f(−x) = −f(x) (symmetric about the origin).
  • Neither: anything else.

Shortcut for polynomials: every term has an even power (a constant counts) → even. Every term has an odd power → odd. A mix → neither.

Example
h(x) = 4x⁷ − x³ + 9x
h(−x) = −4x⁷ + x³ − 9x = −h(x)
Odd, because h(−x) = −h(x). Show the substitution on the exam. The shortcut alone is not a justification.

x³ + x² is neither: odd and even powers mixed.

Factoring

  1. GCF first. Always.
  2. Difference of squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
  3. Keep going until nothing factors. A sum of squares does not factor.

Exponents that are even are perfect squares: y⁶ = (y³)², x⁴ = (x²)².

Example
8x² − 50y⁴ = 2(4x² − 25y⁴)
= 2(2x + 5y²)(2x − 5y²)
Multiply back to check. If you skip the GCF, you get (…)(…) with leftover common factors and lose the point.

Dividing: remainder theorem and synthetic

Remainder Theorem. The remainder when f(x) is divided by (x − k) is f(k). Plug in the number that makes the divisor zero. If f(k) = 0, then (x − k) is a factor.
Example
f(x) = 2x³ + x² − 5 divided by (x + 1): k = −1.
f(−1) = 2(−1)³ + (−1)² − 5 = −2 + 1 − 5 = −6
Put negatives in parentheses. (−1)² = 1, not −1.

Synthetic division (divisor x − k): write the coefficients (use 0 for missing powers), bring down the first, then multiply by k and add, over and over.

(x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6) ÷ (x + 3), so k = −3:

−312−5−6
−336
1−1−20

Quotient x² − x − 2, remainder 0. The last number is the remainder, the others are the quotient (one degree lower).

All the roots, given one

  1. Divide by (x − root) with synthetic division. The remainder must be 0.
  2. Factor the quotient. If it won't factor, use the quadratic formula.
  3. List every root, including the one you were given.
Example
x³ − 2x² − 5x + 6 has root 1.
11−2−56
1−1−6
1−1−60
x² − x − 6 = (x − 3)(x + 2). The roots are 1, 3 and −2.

Solve for a variable

Undo what was done to the variable, in reverse order: clear the fraction, move other terms, then divide by the coefficient.

Example  P = (a + 3b + c) / 5, solve for b.
5P = a + 3b + c
5P − a − c = 3b
b = (5P − a − c) / 3
Subtract the whole group, not just one term. Divide everything by 3.

Average rate of change

(f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a)

It is the slope of the line between the points at x = a and x = b. Subtract in the same order on top and bottom.

Example  f(x) = x³ − x on [−1, 2]
f(2) = 6, f(−1) = −1 + 1 = 0, so (6 − 0)/(2 − (−1)) = 2.
Ordering rates. Find each one and show the work. Then order by absolute value, because the size of the rate is what counts. Rates of 4, 1 and −3 order as 4, −3, 1 since 4 > |−3| = 3 > 1. The negative sign only tells you the function is decreasing.

Open-response checklist

  • Box every final answer.
  • Write explanations in complete sentences.
  • Justify with the rule: "odd multiplicity, because the graph crosses the axis at that zero."
  • Show each step of synthetic division and algebra, even if you can do it in your head.
  • Check: zeros plugged back in give 0, and the sketch matches the end behavior.
